# Content organization and expression

ID: `knowledge.content-expression`. Layer: L1. Related: `visual.voice`, `visual.emphasis`, `goal.clarity`. Nature: design-system practice and ByTaste interpretation.

## Entry points and mechanism

"Everything seems equally important," "template-like copy," "not enough narrative," and "too much text" may concern priorities, tone, sequence, or length. Text and assets are part of composition; changing containers and colors alone cannot resolve confused content roles.

Ask, for example: "Would you rather state the point directly, or present an image or scene before explaining it?" Follow an existing choice directly. The answer does not determine page count, business workflow, or a font.

## Concepts and candidate approaches

| Concept | Relationship to establish | Candidates and boundaries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Content roles | What the subject, primary information, support, metadata, and actions do | Assign roles from existing content; without a product, use guideline categories, specimens, and parameters |
| Semantic grouping and priority | What must be understood together, and what is subordinate or parallel | Write content relationships before spatial placement; visual size does not automatically determine semantic importance |
| Narrative order | Overview, progressive explanation, parallel comparison, or alternating imagery/text | Choose for expression; do not make every page a long scrolling story |
| Voice and tone | Stable language character and context-dependent tone | A brand can be approachable while errors stay precise, without celebratory language |
| Content shape | Distribution of short/long headings, paragraphs, figures, lists, and image regions | Use real or representative lengths; do not invent achievements, customers, or features for layout |
| Clarity and trust | Whether state, capability, consequences, and important limits are understandable | Add only what supports decisions; disclaimers do not fix misleading claims or make unknowns credible |

## Guidelines and previews

Record roles, sequence, and tone examples without another duplicate copy guideline. Describe typical lengths and growth behavior without forcing every heading to have equal length.

Example recommendation: "In the guideline overview, show the actual font specimen before its role and parameters. Explanations concern only the current choice. Long names wrap; secondary parameters do not crowd the specimen." This affects layout and typography and usually requires no content token.

Use varied, valid sample lengths to check priorities, rhythm, and tone. Liking one wording choice does not establish approval of page architecture. Persuasion or conversion measurement is not required.

For AI or uncertain results, distinguish recommendations, executed actions, failures, and unconfirmed facts; see [experience quality](experience-quality.md#clear-expression-and-trustworthy-transparency). Add explanations of correction or evidence only when they affect understanding or decisions, avoiding irrelevant notices.

## Sources and counterexamples

[Atlassian Voice and tone](https://atlassian.design/foundations/content/voice-tone) distinguishes stable voice from contextual tone. ByTaste uses that distinction without adopting its brand personality. [IBM layout](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/layout/) connects content strategy and composition; its whitespace and expression are not universal requirements.

Misuse: adding eyebrow headings, slogans, and explanatory paragraphs to look "designed"; inventing a product story to fill unknown content; removing necessary labels because the user wants concise copy. Preserve unknowns and show the guidelines themselves.
